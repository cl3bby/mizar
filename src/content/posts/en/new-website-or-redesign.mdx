When a site looks tired, the instinct is to start again. Sometimes that is right. Often it is more work and more money than the situation actually needs. Three questions usually settle it.

## 1. Is the content still accurate?

If the services, prices, staff and contact details are correct, and the problem is only how they look, a redesign is usually enough. If the content has drifted for years — closed locations, old phone numbers, services you no longer offer — a rebuild is a chance to fix the foundation rather than paint over it.

## 2. Is the platform still supported?

A site built on an abandoned page builder or an old plugin stack becomes a liability. Security updates stop, things break quietly, and every change costs more than it should. In that case, rebuilding on something simple and static is usually cheaper over the next three years than patching what is there.

## 3. Does it work on a phone?

Check it on your own phone, on mobile data, not office wifi. If the menu is hard to open, the text is tiny, or the page takes more than a few seconds to show anything, that is a structural problem. A cosmetic refresh will not fix it.

## The honest answer

If the answer to all three is good, refresh the design. If two or more are bad, rebuild.

A rebuild does not have to mean losing what you have earned. Existing page addresses can be kept, content can be migrated, and search visibility can be protected with redirects. The point is to end up with something you can maintain, not to start from zero for its own sake.
