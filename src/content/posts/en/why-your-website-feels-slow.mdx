Most slow regional business websites are not slow because the hosting is cheap. They are slow because of what has been added on top of the site over the years.

A typical case looks like this: a page builder, three plugins nobody remembers installing, a chat widget, two analytics scripts, a Facebook pixel, a font loaded from a third party, and a hero image that is still a 4 MB JPEG from 2019. Individually each one is small. Together they turn a page that should load in under a second into one that takes six.

## Where the weight actually comes from

Images are usually the biggest single culprit. A photo straight off a phone or a camera can be ten times larger than it needs to be for a web page. Compressed and correctly sized, the same image can look identical and weigh 90% less.

Scripts are the second. Every third-party widget has to load, run and often fetch something else before the page settles. On a good city connection you barely notice. On a weak regional mobile signal, you do.

Fonts matter too. Loading four weights from a third-party font service means a separate connection to a different domain before text renders properly. Self-hosting one or two weights avoids that round trip entirely.

## What it costs you

A slow site is not just annoying. It is a tax on every enquiry. People on a phone in a paddock or a workshop do not wait around. If the page has not shown them anything useful in a few seconds, they go back to search and tap the next result.

The fix is rarely a new hosting plan. It is removing what does not need to be there, compressing what does, and building the page so the useful part arrives first.
